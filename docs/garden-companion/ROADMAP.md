# Garden Companion Evolution Roadmap

Building a Companion That Grows Alongside the Garden

Version: 1.0

## Purpose

This document defines the long-term evolution of Garden Companion.

It is not a feature backlog.

It is not a sprint plan.

It is the strategic roadmap describing what Garden Companion should become over the next decade.

Every feature should support one of the stages below.

If a feature does not move Garden Companion toward one of these stages, reconsider whether it belongs.

## The Guiding Principle

Garden Companion should not become more complicated.

It should become more understanding.

Every release should make the companion feel more observant, more knowledgeable, and more personal.

The user should never feel like they are learning new software.

They should feel like Garden Companion is learning them.

## Stage One

### Remember

Goal: Build the garden's permanent memory.

Garden Companion learns to remember.

Capabilities:

- Plant profiles
- Tree profiles
- Garden beds
- QR identities
- Photos
- Notes
- Planting history
- Timeline events
- Basic diagnostics
- Task reminders
- Weather snapshots
- Harvest records

Success Criteria:

Garden Companion can accurately answer:

"What happened?"

without losing important history.

## Stage Two

### Understand

Goal: Connect information.

Garden Companion begins recognizing relationships.

Capabilities:

- Companion planting awareness
- Soil awareness
- Seasonal comparisons
- Disease history
- Garden bed relationships
- Growth trends
- Plant families
- User preferences
- Historical comparisons

Success Criteria:

Garden Companion begins answering:

"Why did this happen?"

instead of simply documenting events.

## Stage Three

### Learn

Goal: Recognize patterns over time.

Garden Companion starts learning the unique personality of the garden.

Capabilities:

- Seasonal trend detection
- Pollinator observations
- Pest cycles
- Bloom predictions
- Harvest forecasting
- Watering habits
- Gardening habits
- Preferred user workflows
- Property-specific recommendations

Success Criteria:

Garden Companion begins saying:

"I've noticed..."

before the user asks.

## Stage Four

### Anticipate

Goal: Become proactive.

Garden Companion notices opportunities before problems develop.

Capabilities:

- Weather-triggered alerts
- Disease risk predictions
- Maintenance reminders
- Frost preparation
- Watering suggestions
- Companion planting opportunities
- Plant stress detection
- Seasonal planning

Success Criteria:

Garden Companion prevents problems instead of reacting to them.

## Stage Five

### Collaborate

Goal: Become a true gardening partner.

Garden Companion works with the user rather than responding to requests.

Capabilities:

- Seasonal planning sessions
- Garden design suggestions
- Multi-year crop rotation
- Pollinator planning
- Long-term property improvement
- Plant replacement planning
- Budget-conscious recommendations
- Goal tracking

Success Criteria:

Users begin planning their gardens together with Garden Companion.

## Stage Six

### Stewardship

Goal: Expand beyond gardening.

Garden Companion understands the entire living property.

Capabilities:

- Trees
- Shrubs
- Native habitats
- Wildlife observations
- Pollinator ecosystems
- Orchard management
- Food forests
- Water management
- Biodiversity tracking

The companion evolves from gardening intelligence into property stewardship intelligence.

## Stage Seven

### Legacy

Goal: Preserve gardens across generations.

Capabilities:

- Multi-generation history
- Family memories
- Historical timelines
- Property ownership history
- Legacy trees
- Memorial gardens
- Long-term growth visualizations
- Decades of accumulated knowledge

Garden Companion becomes the permanent memory of the property itself.

## Marketplace Evolution

The marketplace should support stewardship rather than distract from it.

Products should appear because they solve genuine needs.

Marketplace growth should follow user trust, not lead it.

Garden Companion should never feel like it exists to sell products.

## AI Evolution

Year One: Garden Companion answers questions.

Year Two: Garden Companion remembers.

Year Three: Garden Companion recognizes patterns.

Year Five: Garden Companion anticipates needs.

Year Ten: Garden Companion understands the unique personality of the garden.

## Municipal Alignment

Although Garden Companion serves homeowners, its architecture should remain compatible with ArborDex.

Shared concepts should include:

- Plants
- Trees
- QR identities
- Timelines
- Photos
- Diagnostics
- Stewardship
- Historical records

The interfaces differ.

The philosophy remains the same.

## Every Feature Must Strengthen One of These

- Memory
- Understanding
- Context
- Trust
- Stewardship

If it strengthens none of these, it probably does not belong.

## Things We Will Delay

Not every good idea belongs in the present.

Delay features that introduce unnecessary complexity before Garden Companion has earned the user's trust.

Examples include:

- Social networking
- Gamification
- Advertising
- Competitive leaderboards
- Trend-chasing AI features

The foundation always comes first.

## The Experience We Want

A first-time user should think:

"This is helpful."

After one growing season:

"This remembers everything."

After three seasons:

"This understands my garden."

After ten years:

"I couldn't imagine caring for this property without it."

That progression is the roadmap.

## Beyond Gardening

The long-term vision is larger than plants.

Garden Companion should become a lifelong companion for anyone who cares for living things.

Gardens are simply where that journey begins.

## The Final Destination

One day, Garden Companion should know:

- Every plant
- Every tree
- Every season
- Every success
- Every mistake
- Every experiment
- Every lesson
- Every story

Not because it memorized a database.

Because it has quietly grown alongside the gardener for years.

## The ArborTag Promise

Every decision made in this project should answer one question:

"Will this help someone build a deeper relationship with the living things they care for?"

If the answer is yes, we are moving in the right direction.

If the answer is no, rethink the solution.

Everything we build should honor that promise.
